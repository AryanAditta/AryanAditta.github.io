Undergraduate Thesis · KUET · 2026

Open-Source RFIC Design and Verification of a 28-GHz Two-Stage Class-A SiGe Power Amplifier with Machine-Learning-Assisted Optimization

Research question: How can an open-source-compatible RFIC flow carry a mmWave power amplifier from schematic design toward process-aware and EM-aware realization while preserving gain, matching and stability?
  • Designed and simulated a two-stage 28-GHz Class-A SiGe power amplifier using the IHP SG13G2 130-nm SiGe BiCMOS Open PDK with Qucs-S, ngspice/Xyce, KLayout and EM-aware modeling.
  • Developed a progressive ideal-passive → PDK-aware → EM-aware workflow to study how increasingly realistic passive implementation changes RF performance.
  • The final process-aware configuration achieved approximately 17.4 dB gain at 28 GHz, with S11/S22 near −13 dB and K ≈ 12.
  • Used machine learning as a supporting design-exploration layer while retaining circuit simulation as the primary verification authority.
  • A first-author thesis-derived manuscript has been submitted to ICCIT 2026.
  • The work is continuing toward full-custom layout, DRC/LVS-oriented physical verification, parasitic-aware verification and GDSII-level realization of the 28-GHz PA.

IHP SG13G2 · Simulation-Based · 2026–Present

Systematic Design-Space Generation Across SiGe RF Amplifier Topologies

This project develops a reproducible simulation and data-generation workflow for 27 GHz SiGe RF front-end amplifiers rather than optimizing only one circuit point.

  • Studies four circuit families in one consistent framework: common-emitter PA, cascode PA, common-emitter LNA and cascode LNA.
  • Varies physically meaningful circuit parameters including bias conditions, device sizing/multiplier, RF-choke values and input/output matching-network components.
  • Characterizes candidate configurations through DC verification, small-signal S-parameter analysis, stability checks, noise/noise-figure analysis, impedance matching and large-signal harmonic-balance simulation before Python post-processing.
  • Builds a structured design-response dataset intended for comparative topology analysis and later surrogate/ML-assisted RF design-space exploration.
  • The numerical design-response dataset remains private research data; the public repository exposes selected schematics, methodology and reproducible documentation only.
  • All results are simulation-based and are not presented as fabricated or measured-silicon performance.
