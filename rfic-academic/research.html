Undergraduate Thesis · KUET · 2026

Open-Source RFIC Design & Verification of a 28 GHz Two-Stage Class-A SiGe Power Amplifier

Research question: How can an open-source-compatible RFIC flow carry a mmWave power amplifier from schematic design toward process-aware and EM-aware realization while preserving gain, matching and stability?

I designed a two-stage Class-A power amplifier centered at 28 GHz using the IHP SG13G2 130-nm SiGe BiCMOS Open PDK. The work treats circuit simulation as the primary evidence and progressively introduces more realistic passive and electromagnetic effects.

  • Built the RF circuit and verification flow around Qucs-S with ngspice/Xyce-based simulation and Python-assisted analysis.
  • Progressed from ideal-passive design to PDK-aware components and EM-characterized passive structures using KLayout and openEMS-compatible workflows.
  • Evaluated S-parameters, impedance matching, reverse isolation and unconditional stability across the realization stages.
  • The final process-aware simulation achieved approximately 17.4 dB gain at 28 GHz with S11/S22 near −13 dB and K ≈ 12.
  • Machine learning was used only as a supporting design-space exploration tool rather than as a replacement for circuit simulation or RF verification.

Scope: Simulation-based work. No fabricated-silicon or measured-performance claim is made; future work extends toward full-custom layout, DRC/LVS and extracted/post-layout verification.
