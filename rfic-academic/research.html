Undergraduate Thesis · KUET · 2026

Open-Source RFIC Design and Verification of a 28-GHz Two-Stage Class-A SiGe Power Amplifier with Machine-Learning-Assisted Optimization

Research question: How can an open-source-compatible RFIC flow carry a mmWave power amplifier from schematic design toward process-aware and EM-aware realization while preserving gain, matching and stability?

Designed and simulated a two-stage 28-GHz Class-A SiGe power amplifier using the IHP SG13G2 130-nm SiGe BiCMOS Open PDK and an open-source RFIC workflow based on Qucs-S, ngspice/Xyce, KLayout and EM-aware modeling.

Developed a progressive ideal-passive → PDK-aware → EM-aware realization workflow. The final process-aware configuration achieved approximately 17.4 dB gain at 28 GHz, with S11/S22 near −13 dB and K ≈ 12, allowing the effect of increasingly realistic passive implementation to be evaluated directly.

Used machine learning as a supporting design-exploration layer for candidate screening while retaining circuit simulation as the primary verification authority. The work is being extended toward layout/GDSII-level realization, with a first-author thesis-derived manuscript submitted to ICCIT 2026.

Scope: Simulation-based work. No fabricated-silicon or measured-performance claim is made; the next implementation stage is full-custom layout followed by DRC/LVS and extracted/post-layout verification.

Complementary VLSI/EDA Work

Manuscript SubmittedFirst Author

Learning-Guided VLSI Floorplanning

Independent research on learning-guided physical design for constraint-rich, outline-aware floorplanning. The work combines graph-based circuit representations with B*-tree simulated annealing, structured legalization and exact feasibility checks so that learned guidance improves search without replacing explicit physical-design constraints.

The broader study also examines where direct neural floorplanning succeeds and where discrete structure is still necessary, especially the gap between learning strong global placement geometry and achieving exact local legality. This provides a complementary perspective on ML-assisted EDA: learning is used as a design prior while optimization remains responsible for hard feasibility.

Ongoing Research

Leakage-Aware RTL-to-QoR Prediction

Research collaboration on predicting mapped area and pre-layout timing from RTL before full synthesis and timing analysis. The workflow emphasizes source provenance, duplicate and near-duplicate control and component-aware grouping so model evaluation better reflects generalization to genuinely different RTL designs.

The current direction studies structural-complexity regimes, size-aware prediction, explainability and coverage awareness using RTL-derived features with Yosys and OpenSTA-based flows. The goal is not only to produce early QoR estimates, but also to understand when those estimates are trustworthy and which RTL structures are driving them.
